<objective>
Give the acrylic edge a directional anti-aliasing and make the wet control govern edge mixing, then write down the softness resolution.

UAT row 13 asks for three things in one sentence: a directional smooth on the stroke edges with no aliasing on the border or inside, watery irregular edges that are not ruler-straight, and the wet control stopping edge mixing with Feather setting how much edge mixing happens. The shipped gather already has the pieces — a brush-local streaked band, a screen-space isotropic tooth, a Feather threshold rise and a last-touch wetness field — but the tooth is isotropic, so the fringe's pixel-scale roughness has no preferred direction; the wetness field gates pickup and lift but not the rim's blend depth; and the constant softness function keeps being read as the reason Feather "does nothing".

Purpose: the artist's complaint is about the edge's behaviour, not its width. Directional smoothing is what makes a pixel edge read as a brush edge; the wet gate is what makes the acrylic/water request true; and the softness resolution is what stops a future reader from "fixing" a constant that is deliberately constant.

Output: an anisotropic, tangent-aligned tooth inside `grainHeight` with its constants and pure twin; a wetness-weighted rim mix that reads the existing last-touch field and the tack time; an extended wetness metric with per-side edge support and a same-sheet wet-versus-dried pair; the roughness ceiling that 64-29 deliberately deferred; and the pinned softness resolution.
</objective>
