<objective>
Ship the traces and the directional edges — the laws that make a bristle stroke read as a brush instead of a soft bar, and the census that proves the edge is genuinely directional and feather-controlled.

This is the third slice of the acrylic bristle re-opening (D-26..D-29), and the home of acceptance ask #2 in `SPECS/v2.2.0-medium-engine/better-acrylic.md`. 64-22 deposits the gathered carried coefficient on a coverage mask that is still smooth and ruler-shaped: no dryness breakup, no paper tooth, no rim noise, no staggered landing, no rim bias, and a body that reads local only. This plan adds exactly the six reference laws the notes call "the internal traces" and "the noisy, feathered rim" (§4.3, §4.4, §4.5, §5.1, §5.3, §5.4, §5.5) — nothing else, and never a replacement for the engine's pressure path (§5.2 is explicitly out of scope).

Purpose: this is where the reference look becomes plausible. The traces are the stroke eating itself as its per-bristle load runs out; the tooth is a threshold against paper height, not a texture; the rim is the aggregate of independent per-bristle decisions. And because "the edge looks brushed" is not a byte gate, the plan ships a measurable census: one capture, two strokes identical but for Feather, whose 10 %→90 % transition width and along-stroke crossing variation are reported and relation-checked.
Output: the §4.3–§5.5 constants and their tested pure formulas in `bristles.ts`; the extended `BRISTLE_GATHER` with the shared noise family and the grain-height trio; the carrier's rim/streak uniforms bound from the same constants; the extended structural pins; a `bristle-edge` probe scenario with its invariant; and five baselines (four re-authored bristle gates plus the new census capture).
</objective>
