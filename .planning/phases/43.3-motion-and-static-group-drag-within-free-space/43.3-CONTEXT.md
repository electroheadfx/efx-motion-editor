# Phase 43.3: Motion and Static Group Drag Within Free Space - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-08-13
**Status:** Ready for planning

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

Allow the user to drag a Motion Group or Static Group directly from its integrated Group Rail and move that complete Group inside available empty timeline space without pushing or pulling neighboring content. A source-attached Group moves its complete real source cycle, rail, linked repeat interval, placement, boundaries, and derived preview together; a duplicated placement sharing another source cycle moves only its placement and linked interval and never touches shared source keys. Every interval vacated or newly opened by an accepted drag becomes an intentional persistent empty gap recorded with Phase 43.1 stable-key-owned interpolation-break semantics; Interpolation On never generates frames across it, and the gap survives Interpolation Off/On.

Previous and next timeline objects are collision boundaries: drag clamps at them and never overlaps, reorders, stretches, splits, cuts, or changes Repeat/Infinity. Preview shows the proposed destination, intentional gap, and collision limits before commit. The accepted movement — Group plus any break needed to preserve an opened gap — is one atomic parent-acknowledged transaction with one Undo/Redo, surviving save/reopen.

The accepted canonical physical document, exact parent acknowledgement, source sharing, Phase 43.1 break authority, Phase 43.2 Group lifecycle authority (`visibleRanges`, `phaseOrigin`, `originalEndExclusive`, `frameOverrides`, sync/provenance state), existing key drag, rigid physical-key group drag, Key Spacing, Group Rail selection, inspector, Edit Group, Regenerate, preview/export parity, and save/reopen remain authoritative and unchanged. Key Group rails are Phase 43.4; Push Right/Push Left multi-object movement is Phase 43.5; the Motion Editor timeline is unchanged.

Requirements are not yet assigned IDs in `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md`; downstream research/planning must derive Phase 43.3 IDs and traceability from the activated product prompt without reopening completed Phase 43/43.1/43.2 requirements.

**43.2 carryover caution:** frozen `69d07703` still has open native red loops for project-local save staging, Paint into a deleted Group gap, and original-extent Regenerate. Group drag must build on the accepted Group lifecycle authority without reopening those repair paths, and its own native UAT runs from a fully restarted frozen-code session (43.2 D-39).

</domain>

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Drag preview visuals

- **D-01:** During a rail drag, a translucent mode-colored ghost rail follows the cursor at the proposed destination while the original rail stays visible in place until commit. Nothing presents as moved before exact parent acceptance.
- **D-02:** The interval the Group would vacate, and any newly opened destination interval, preview in the same neutral-gray empty-cell treatment Phase 43.2 locked for deleted Group gaps — one consistent intentional-empty-space language. No distinct drag-gap visual is introduced.
- **D-03:** At a collision boundary the ghost clamps and the blocked edge shows a red/error treatment consistent with the rail's existing red unresolved treatment, making the limit visible before drop.
- **D-04:** The rail gesture starts a drag only after a small movement threshold (~4px). Plain click select, Shift/Cmd multi-select, and double-click Edit Group remain intact. Escape cancels the drag with zero state change and no history entry.

### Collision and drop policy

- **D-05:** Dropping while the ghost is clamped commits at the clamped position — what the preview showed is what gets committed. Rejection happens only when zero valid movement exists in the dragged direction.
- **D-06:** When the Group has no free space in the dragged direction, a mapped status-line message (e.g. `No empty space in that direction.`) is published through the single product-reason mapper pattern from 43.1/43.2. No modal, no sound, no silent snap-back without feedback.
- **D-07:** An accepted move publishes a status line naming the destination and any opened gap: `Moved Group to frame {N}.` plus `Gap left at frames {A}–{B}.` when applicable, following the 43.1 accepted-status precedent.
- **D-08:** Collision boundaries are frame 0, physical capacity/parent end, every previous/next real key, and every other Group interval (including linked occurrences of other Groups). The moved Group's own current interval is pass-through space. Drag never crosses, overlaps, or reorders these boundaries.

### Gap and break recording

- **D-09:** When a source-attached Group vacates its interval, the next real key after the vacated interval receives/owns the incoming interpolation break under exact Phase 43.1 stable-key-owned semantics. An existing break on that key is reused, never duplicated. No standalone frame-range gap record is created. — **Reversibility:** one-way — once persisted, breaks participate in canonical revision/history snapshots; moving ownership off stable key identity would require rewriting saved physical documents and replay semantics.
- **D-10:** When the move opens a new empty interval between the moved Group's first source key and its new predecessor, that first key receives a new incoming break. Landing immediately adjacent to the predecessor creates no break. Breaks already owned by the moved keys travel with their identity unchanged (43.1 D-14) — the drag never silently adds or removes them beyond the new landing-gap break.
- **D-11 (user-refined):** A duplicated shared-source placement move never moves, clones, or retimes shared source keys and never changes breaks owned by those source-key identities merely because the placement moved. However, the placement move may update surrounding stable-key-owned break authority when required to preserve newly vacated or residual empty intervals: the next real key after a newly vacated interval owns or reuses the incoming break; existing destination-gap breaks are preserved and reused; no duplicate break or standalone gap record is created; all placement and break changes commit atomically with one Undo/Redo. If existing breaks already preserve every resulting empty interval, no break change is needed.
- **D-12:** No break is created when no real key would interpolate across a gap: a vacated interval at the end of content (no successor key) or a landing before all content (no predecessor) stays empty by construction.
- **D-13 (user-refined):** Phase 43.2 Group-local deleted phases and `visibleRanges` move rigidly with the Group at their original relative source phases. They remain Group-local authority and must never be converted into neighboring-key interpolation breaks. Only external gaps opened by the drag may update stable-key-owned break authority.

### Resolver intent and interaction scope

- **D-14:** Rail drag enters the physical resolver as a new dedicated Group-drag intent carrying Group identity and destination. The source-attached case reuses the proven rigid whole-cycle translation math and `placementStart` follow; the duplicated case is placement-only. `move-key-group` stays anchored on grabbed physical keys and remains unchanged for cell-level drags. — **Reversibility:** costly — a dedicated intent joins the closed transport union, canonical serialization, coordinator payloads, and history replay; merging it into `move-key-group` later would touch every producer and fixture.
- **D-15:** Rail drag (whole Group, free-space clamp, gap/break recording) and physical-key group drag (rigid translation of a selected key set) coexist as two gestures with separate contracts. Physical group drag behavior is a locked regression boundary and stays unchanged.
- **D-16:** This phase produces a UI-SPEC via `/gsd-ui-phase 43.3` after context: the ghost rail, clamped red blocked edge, neutral-gray gap preview, gesture threshold, and status copy are a new visual/interaction surface on the rail (43-UI-SPEC.md precedent).
- **D-17:** After an accepted drag, the moved Group stays selected, the physical cursor stays where it is, and the Studio canvas reconciles from the accepted document when the current frame's content changed. No automatic navigation to the moved Group (43.2 D-23 / 43 D-55 precedent).

### Locked guards (from the activated product prompt, restated as decisions)

- **D-18:** Clamp or reject any movement that crosses frame 0, exceeds physical capacity/parent bounds, collides with another object, creates duplicate real-key destinations, breaks stable identity order, or leaves any Group unresolved. Reject stale or ambiguous Group, source, or interpolation-break authority before mutation. A failed or cancelled drag changes nothing and creates no history entry.
- **D-19:** A frame inside a Group targets the complete Group. Direct drag never stretches, splits, cuts, or changes Repeat/Infinity, and never materializes linked occurrences or duplicates source assets.
- **D-20:** Key Group rails (Phase 43.4), Push Right/Push Left (Phase 43.5), internal multi-track Paint, and Motion Editor timeline changes are out of scope.

### Claude's Discretion

- Exact ghost translucency, red blocked-edge geometry, and gray gap preview rendering within existing rail/cell tokens, provided zero added row/track height and no confusion with selection orange, unresolved red on the accepted rail, or canonical white endpoint cuts.
- Exact ~4px threshold value, pointer capture mechanics, and cursor styles, provided click/Shift/Cmd/double-click gestures and Escape cancellation stay intact.
- Exact intent name and additive payload shape for the Group-drag intent, provided it joins the closed union with strict parsing, canonical revision participation, and fail-closed resolver validation.
- Exact status-copy wording beyond the locked destination-plus-gap facts, provided one mapper owns disabled, rejection, and acceptance copy.
- Exact test decomposition. Native visual UAT remains required for ghost/clamp preview, gap persistence across Interpolation Off/On, duplicated-placement independence, Undo/Redo, and save/reopen.

</decisions>

## Existing Code Insights

### Reusable Assets

- `PhysicsPaintWorkflowStrip.tsx` already implements a complete physical-key drag stack: gesture session with candidate target/kind tracking, preview view-model, vacated-cell diffing, publication staging, and rejection callbacks (`onRotoGroupDragRejected`). The rail drag reuses this preview/publication pattern, not the key-anchored target resolution.
- The resolver's `move-key-group` path (physicsPaintRotoPhysicalResolver.ts ~L1645+) already proves rigid whole-cycle translation with a single physical delta, `nextLoopClips` placementStart follow for source-attached loops, and duplicated placements keeping `placementStart` — the exact movement algebra the source-attached rail drag needs.
- The 43.1 break machinery (stable-key-owned incoming breaks in model/resolver/persistence) is the recording mechanism for drag-opened gaps; no new gap authority is needed.
- The 43.1/43.2 product-reason mapper pattern owns disabled preflight and rejection copy; the no-space rejection and accepted-move status plug into it.
- Phase 43 D-41 reserved drag-compatible pointer geometry on the rail; this phase activates it without changing rail geometry.

### Established Patterns

- The parent-authoritative physical document is accepted truth: ghost/preview state is presentation-only; the Group, keys, breaks, and rail never present as moved before exact correlated acknowledgement.
- Additive persistent outcomes (moved keys, new breaks, moved placements) participate together in strict parsing, canonical revision fingerprints, bridge equality, persistence, and complete history snapshots.
- Preact Signals/computed projections carry drag preview state; no effect-driven mirrored state or prop-drilling sprawl; keep new logic out of `PhysicsPaintStudio.tsx` where a focused module suffices.
- Linked occurrences remain compact virtual modulo resolutions; the drag never materializes repeat destinations or creates a second cache authority.
- Native visual UAT is the final oracle and runs from a fully restarted frozen-code parent/child session.

### Integration Points

- Rail component: pointer-down/move/up drag session with move threshold, Escape cancel, ghost presentation, clamp computation against collision boundaries, and red blocked-edge treatment.
- Resolver: new dedicated Group-drag intent — Group identity + destination; derives source-cycle rigid translation (source-attached) or placement-only update (duplicated), computes clamps, validates duplicate-destination/identity-order/capacity guards, and derives required break additions/reuse.
- Coordinator/history/bridge: one atomic transaction carrying complete records, Loop Clips, incoming breaks, selection, and cursor; one Undo/Redo restores/reapplies movement and movement-created breaks together.
- Persistence/preview/export/save: moved placements, exact gaps, breaks, synchronization status, and source sharing resolve identically across Studio, preview, export, and save/reopen.

## Specific Ideas

- Ghost rail: translucent, mode-colored, follows the cursor; original rail stays visible in place until commit.
- Gap preview: neutral gray, identical to 43.2 deleted-gap cells.
- Collision: ghost clamps at the boundary; blocked edge shows red, consistent with the rail's existing unresolved red.
- No-space rejection copy: `No empty space in that direction.` (mapped product reason).
- Accepted-move copy: `Moved Group to frame {N}.` plus `Gap left at frames {A}–{B}.` when a gap opened.
- Drag threshold ~4px; Escape cancels with zero state change and no history entry.
- Break ownership after move: next real key after a vacated interval owns/reuses the incoming break; moved Group's first source key gets a break only when a landing gap actually opens.
- User-locked refinement: Group-local deleted phases/`visibleRanges` travel rigidly with the Group at original relative source phases and never convert into key-owned breaks; only external drag-opened gaps may update stable-key-owned break authority.
- User-locked refinement: duplicated placements may still require surrounding break updates for newly vacated/residual intervals — but if existing breaks already preserve every resulting empty interval, no break change occurs.

</specifics>
