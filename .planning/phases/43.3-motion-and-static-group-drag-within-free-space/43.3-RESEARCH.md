## User Constraints (from CONTEXT.md)

### Locked Decisions

**Drag preview visuals**

- **D-01:** During a rail drag, a translucent mode-colored ghost rail follows the cursor at the proposed destination while the original rail stays visible in place until commit. Nothing presents as moved before exact parent acceptance.
- **D-02:** The interval the Group would vacate, and any newly opened destination interval, preview in the same neutral-gray empty-cell treatment Phase 43.2 locked for deleted Group gaps — one consistent intentional-empty-space language. No distinct drag-gap visual is introduced.
- **D-03:** At a collision boundary the ghost clamps and the blocked edge shows a red/error treatment consistent with the rail's existing red unresolved treatment, making the limit visible before drop.
- **D-04:** The rail gesture starts a drag only after a small movement threshold (~4px). Plain click select, Shift/Cmd multi-select, and double-click Edit Group remain intact. Escape cancels the drag with zero state change and no history entry.

**Collision and drop policy**

- **D-05:** Dropping while the ghost is clamped commits at the clamped position — what the preview showed is what gets committed. Rejection happens only when zero valid movement exists in the dragged direction.
- **D-06:** When the Group has no free space in the dragged direction, a mapped status-line message (e.g. `No empty space in that direction.`) is published through the single product-reason mapper pattern from 43.1/43.2. No modal, no sound, no silent snap-back without feedback.
- **D-07:** An accepted move publishes a status line naming the destination and any opened gap: `Moved Group to frame {N}.` plus `Gap left at frames {A}–{B}.` when applicable, following the 43.1 accepted-status precedent.
- **D-08:** Collision boundaries are frame 0, physical capacity/parent end, every previous/next real key, and every other Group interval (including linked occurrences of other Groups). The moved Group's own current interval is pass-through space. Drag never crosses, overlaps, or reorders these boundaries.

**Gap and break recording**

- **D-09:** When a source-attached Group vacates its interval, the next real key after the vacated interval receives/owns the incoming interpolation break under exact Phase 43.1 stable-key-owned semantics. An existing break on that key is reused, never duplicated. No standalone frame-range gap record is created. — **Reversibility:** one-way.
- **D-10:** When the move opens a new empty interval between the moved Group's first source key and its new predecessor, that first key receives a new incoming break. Landing immediately adjacent to the predecessor creates no break. Breaks already owned by the moved keys travel with their identity unchanged (43.1 D-14) — the drag never silently adds or removes them beyond the new landing-gap break.
- **D-11 (user-refined):** A duplicated shared-source placement move never moves, clones, or retimes shared source keys and never changes breaks owned by those source-key identities merely because the placement moved. However, the placement move may update surrounding stable-key-owned break authority when required to preserve newly vacated or residual empty intervals: the next real key after a newly vacated interval owns or reuses the incoming break; existing destination-gap breaks are preserved and reused; no duplicate break or standalone gap record is created; all placement and break changes commit atomically with one Undo/Redo. If existing breaks already preserve every resulting empty interval, no break change is needed.
- **D-12:** No break is created when no real key would interpolate across a gap: a vacated interval at the end of content (no successor key) or a landing before all content (no predecessor) stays empty by construction.
- **D-13 (user-refined):** Phase 43.2 Group-local deleted phases and `visibleRanges` move rigidly with the Group at their original relative source phases. They remain Group-local authority and must never be converted into neighboring-key interpolation breaks. Only external gaps opened by the drag may update stable-key-owned break authority.

**Resolver intent and interaction scope**

- **D-14:** Rail drag enters the physical resolver as a new dedicated Group-drag intent carrying Group identity and destination. The source-attached case reuses the proven rigid whole-cycle translation math and `placementStart` follow; the duplicated case is placement-only. `move-key-group` stays anchored on grabbed physical keys and remains unchanged for cell-level drags. — **Reversibility:** costly.
- **D-15:** Rail drag (whole Group, free-space clamp, gap/break recording) and physical-key group drag (rigid translation of a selected key set) coexist as two gestures with separate contracts. Physical group drag behavior is a locked regression boundary and stays unchanged.
- **D-16:** This phase produces a UI-SPEC via `/gsd-ui-phase 43.3` after context — DONE: `43.3-UI-SPEC.md` approved 2026-08-13.
- **D-17:** After an accepted drag, the moved Group stays selected, the physical cursor stays where it is, and the Studio canvas reconciles from the accepted document when the current frame's content changed. No automatic navigation to the moved Group (43.2 D-23 / 43 D-55 precedent).

**Locked guards (restated as decisions)**

- **D-18:** Clamp or reject any movement that crosses frame 0, exceeds physical capacity/parent bounds, collides with another object, creates duplicate real-key destinations, breaks stable identity order, or leaves any Group unresolved. Reject stale or ambiguous Group, source, or interpolation-break authority before mutation. A failed or cancelled drag changes nothing and creates no history entry.
- **D-19:** A frame inside a Group targets the complete Group. Direct drag never stretches, splits, cuts, or changes Repeat/Infinity, and never materializes linked occurrences or duplicates source assets.
- **D-20:** Key Group rails (Phase 43.4), Push Right/Push Left (Phase 43.5), internal multi-track Paint, and Motion Editor timeline changes are out of scope.

## Summary

Phase 43.3 adds a whole-Group pointer drag gesture on the existing integrated Group Rail: drag a Motion or Static Group into free timeline space, clamp at collision boundaries, and record any vacated or newly opened interval as a persistent intentional gap via the existing Phase 43.1 stable-key-owned interpolation-break machinery. The phase is a **pure in-repo extension of an already-closed architecture** — no new packages, no new external services, no new persisted schema fields. Three existing, proven mechanisms supply nearly all of the movement algebra: (1) the resolver's rigid-translation + `placementStart`-follow math (`buildMoveGroupCandidate` / `computeSourceAttachedLoopPlacementFollow`), (2) the 43.1 incoming-break recording pipeline that already flows through resolver → proposal → apply payload → canonical revision → history, and (3) the strip's complete pointer-drag session idiom (threshold, capture, Escape, edge scroll, publication staging, focus restoration).

The genuinely new work is concentrated in four places: (1) a new dedicated intent kind in the closed union (`types/physicPaint.ts`) carrying Group identity + destination, with clamp-and-commit semantics that differ fundamentally from `move-key-group`'s reject-on-collision; (2) a new resolver branch that derives source-attached rigid translation or duplicated placement-only movement, computes free-space clamps against all collision boundaries, and derives required break additions/reuse; (3) a rail-side drag session in `PhysicsPaintLoopClipRail.tsx` that arms past a 4px threshold without breaking the existing click/double-click timer machinery; and (4) ghost/gap/blocked-edge preview rendering as presentation-only Signal state, plus the two new mapped status lines.

**Primary recommendation:** Implement the Group-drag intent as a new additive member of `PhysicPaintRotoPhysicalEditIntent` (D-14) whose resolver branch reuses `computeSourceAttachedLoopPlacementFollow` for the source-attached case and emits placement-only `nextLoopClips` plus computed `nextIncomingInterpolationBreakKeyIds` for both cases; wire the rail gesture through a new prepare/commit pair in `useRotoTimelineActions.ts` modeled byte-closely on `prepareRotoKeyGroupDrag`/`commitRotoKeyGroupDrag`, and keep every preview element as session-only Signal state that never touches canonical revision, history, or persistence.

### Pattern 2: Rigid translation + placement-follow reuse

**What:** The source-attached rail drag reuses the proven whole-cycle math.
**Verified:** `computeSourceAttachedLoopPlacementFollow` [VERIFIED: physicsPaintRotoPhysicalResolver.ts:859-919] docstring, verbatim:

> "D-04 rigid whole-cycle drag placement follow (placement/source correction): a loop's placementStart tracks the drag ONLY when the moved set contains the cycle's ENTIRE source key list AND the loop's placementStart coincided with the cycle's pre-move first key frame (an original loop). It also rebuilds finite lifecycle extent (`phaseOrigin`, `originalEndExclusive`, single-range `visibleRanges`) when authorized spacing retimes the cycle (resolver:881-912) — the rail drag needs the same lifecycle-field rewrite when a source-attached Group's placement moves, keeping Group-local deleted phases at original relative source phases (D-13). The drag arm therefore must: replace the bare `stopPointerEvent` with a real pointer-down that starts session tracking; on threshold crossing, cancel the pending single-click timer (`clearClickSequence`) and suppress the post-drop `click` event (the physical-key drag already solves the same problem with `suppressNextRotoClickRef` [VERIFIED: PhysicsPaintWorkflowStrip.tsx:1192, 1245, 1254]); on Escape/cancel before threshold, leave the click path untouched.

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Merging the rail drag into `move-key-group`:** its semantics are reject-on-collision with a grabbed-key anchor; rail drag is clamp-and-commit with a Group anchor (D-05/D-14/D-15). Merging corrupts a locked regression boundary.
- **Optimistic rail movement:** the accepted rail never translates/dims before exact parent acknowledgement (D-01). Ghost is a separate paint-only element.
- **A standalone frame-range gap record:** gaps are recorded ONLY as stable-key-owned incoming breaks (D-09). No new persisted field, no second gap authority.
- **Effect-driven preview mirroring:** ghost/clamp state is Signal-driven session view state updated from pointer handlers, per CLAUDE.md no-effect-control-flow directive.
- **Routing commit through `runPhysicalAction`:** its kind union is a bounded `Extract` [VERIFIED: useRotoTimelineActions.ts:519-522]; drag commits use the publication idiom.
- **New rows/geometry/UI surfaces:** zero added row/track height, no handles/grips/icons (UI-SPEC geometry contract; physics-paint-UI-spec-fidelity memory).

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Proposal version fingerprint omits break state
**What goes wrong:** The existing drag prepare paths call `buildProposalVersion(records, interpolation, loopClips, launch)` [VERIFIED: useRotoTimelineActions.ts:1398-1406], which delegates to `buildPhysicPaintRotoPhysicalRevision(records, interpolation, loopClips)` **without passing the break collection** — the revision builder's fourth parameter defaults to empty [VERIFIED: physicsPaintRotoPhysicalModel.ts:947-956]:
```typescript
export function buildPhysicPaintRotoPhysicalRevision(
  records: unknown,
  interpolation: unknown,
  loopClips: unknown,
  incomingInterpolationBreakKeyIds: unknown = PHYSIC_PAINT_ROTO_INCOMING_INTERPOLATION_BREAK_KEY_IDS_EMPTY,
  groupOverrideRecords: unknown = [],
): string {
```
**Why it happens:** Existing move intents never change breaks (breaks travel with identity, 43.1 D-14), so omission was safe. The rail drag DOES add breaks.
**How to avoid:** The new prepare must pass `input.getIncomingInterpolationBreakKeyIds?.()` both into the resolver input (the resolver already accepts it, resolver:149-150) and into the revision fingerprint, so stale/ambiguous break authority rejects before mutation (D-18). Do NOT change the existing single/group-key prepare call sites — regression boundary.
**Warning signs:** a redo/stale-check passing when break authority changed between prepare and commit.

### Pitfall 2: Post-drop click re-fires rail selection or Edit Group
**What goes wrong:** After pointer capture + drop, the browser still dispatches `click` on the capture target; the rail's click handler would run its selection/double-click timer logic after an accepted drag.
**Why it happens:** The rail click path is timer-based (250ms deferral) and independent of pointer sessions.
**How to avoid:** Reuse the strip's `suppressNextRotoClickRef` idiom: set suppression at drag begin, clear via `setTimeout(0)` after pointerup, and have the rail click handler bail when suppressed. Also cancel the pending single-click timer when the threshold crosses.
**Warning signs:** selection changing or the Edit Group dialog opening right after a completed drag.

### Pitfall 3: Clamping vs rejecting — wrong semantic at the resolver
**What goes wrong:** Implementing collision as a rejection (like `move-key-group`'s `duplicate-destination-frame`) instead of clamping the destination to the nearest free position and committing it (D-05).
**Why it happens:** `buildMoveGroupCandidate` rejects on any unselected-key collision; copying it blindly imports the wrong semantic.
**How to avoid:** The clamp belongs in the new intent's destination computation: candidate placement = clamp(proposed, nearest boundary in drag direction); rejection occurs only when zero valid movement exists in that direction (D-06). The UI ghost must show exactly the clamped value the resolver will commit — preview is the commit contract.
**Warning signs:** native UAT row 4 ("Preview clamps or rejects at neighboring content") failing with silent snap-back.

### Pitfall 4: Duplicated-placement drag accidentally moves shared source keys
**What goes wrong:** Treating a duplicated placement like a source-attached Group translates keys shared with other Groups, violating D-11 and corrupting the other placements.
**Why it happens:** The source-attached detection requires BOTH "moved set contains the entire source cycle" AND "placementStart coincided with the pre-move first key frame" (resolver:875-878) — a rail drag carries no grabbed keys, so the intent must determine attachment from the Group record itself: source-attached iff `placementStart === framesByKeyId.get(sourceKeyIds[0])` at preparation time (the same coincidence test), validated fail-closed against ambiguity.
**How to avoid:** Encode attachment as an explicit, resolver-validated derivation — never a UI-supplied flag. Duplicated case emits key mapping identical to input plus placement-only `nextLoopClips`.
**Warning signs:** UAT row 5/10 failure; shared source cycle keys changing frames.

### Pitfall 5: Ghost preview leaking into canonical state or history
**What goes wrong:** Preview intervals (vacated/destination gaps) persisted, revisioned, or replayed.
**Why it happens:** Preview built by mutating shared document-shaped structures instead of session-only Signals.
**How to avoid:** Preview state lives in a drag-session Signal consumed only for paint (class application + ghost element), exactly like `rotoDragPreview`/`rotoDragVacatedAppFrames`; commit uses only the retained immutable publication (D-09 idiom).
**Warning signs:** revision/hash changes during pointer movement; Undo capturing a preview state.

### Pitfall 6: Lifecycle fields and `visibleRanges` mistranslated on source-attached moves
**What goes wrong:** A source-attached Group with Group-local deleted phases (multi-range `visibleRanges`, `frameOverrides`) collapses to one full range or loses override frames when moved.
**Why it happens:** The existing retime path in `computeSourceAttachedLoopPlacementFollow` rebuilds `visibleRanges` as a single full range (resolver:905-909) — correct for Key Spacing, wrong for a rigid move that must preserve relative deleted phases (D-13).
**How to avoid:** The rail-drag branch must translate `phaseOrigin`, `originalEndExclusive`, every `visibleRanges` entry, and every `frameOverrides` `appFrame` by the SAME signed delta, preserving relative offsets verbatim. Lifecycle field shapes [VERIFIED: physicsPaintRotoPhysicalModel.ts:266-282]:
```typescript
export interface PhysicPaintRotoLoopClip {
  readonly loopId: string;
  readonly placementStart: number;
  readonly sourceKeyIds: readonly string[];
  readonly repeat: number | 'infinity';
  readonly mode: 'progressive' | 'static';
  readonly scriptId?: string;
  readonly motion?: PhysicPaintRotoScriptMotionSettings;
  readonly overrideColor?: string | null;
  /** Durable Group lifecycle facts. Canonical finite Groups always carry all six members. */
  readonly syncState?: 'synchronized' | 'modified';
  readonly provenanceState?: 'attached' | 'detached';
  readonly phaseOrigin?: number;
  readonly originalEndExclusive?: number;
  readonly visibleRanges?: readonly PhysicPaintRotoGroupVisibleRange[];
  readonly frameOverrides?: readonly PhysicPaintRotoGroupFrameOverride[];
}
```
**Warning signs:** deleted Group occurrences reappearing after a drag; synchronization status flipping to Modified spuriously (UAT row 8).

### Pitfall 7: Infinity and 0f Groups at the extremes
**What goes wrong:** An Infinity Group's `effectiveEnd` tracks `parentEndExclusive` dynamically (resolver D-25 comment, VERIFIED at resolver:3057-3060); dragging one right shrinks its derived occurrence count — which must NOT be treated as a Repeat change (D-19) but DOES change the effective interval used for clamp/preview. A 0f-effective Group (boundary at placement start) has a zero-width interval that clamp math must not divide by or skip.
**How to avoid:** Compute the dragged interval from the derived range record (`phaseOrigin` → resolved `effectiveEnd`, the same projection the rail already draws, rail:181-195), clamp by interval overlap, and handle zero-width intervals explicitly. Confirm the Infinity rightward-drag semantics against the derived-extent precedent (43 native acceptance already treats next-clip moves as legitimately re-shaping an Infinity loop's effective end).
**Warning signs:** ghost width flickering for Infinity Groups; 0f Group undraggable or jumping.

### Pitfall 8: Reopening 43.2's frozen repair paths
**What goes wrong:** Touching save staging, Paint-into-gap, or Regenerate original-extent code "while nearby."
**Why it happens:** Those red loops live in the same coordinator/lifecycle modules.
**How to avoid:** CONTEXT carryover caution is explicit — build on accepted lifecycle authority only; keep `[SAVE-REOPEN]` and the three red loops out of scope; any incidental discovery routes to a new quick task, not this phase.
**Warning signs:** diff touching the 43.2 frozen candidate's repair surface.
