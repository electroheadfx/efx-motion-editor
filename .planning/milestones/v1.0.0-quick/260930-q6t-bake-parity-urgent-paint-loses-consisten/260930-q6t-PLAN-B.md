Quantify the actual loss mechanism behind the bake-parity regression (260930-q6t), after Task 1 refuted the /800-vs-Beer-Lambert premise (dry transfer is denser, never lighter — cannot cause lightening).

User direction (locked, from the oracle's 2 screenshots: medium/dark navy pigment on light paper): the symptom is "lighter" AND "lost consistency" together = **LOST MASS**, not an alpha rescale. Prime suspect is physics-drifted wet sitting outside `dryRegionForStroke`, never transferred by the bbox-scoped `forceDryAll`, then destroyed when the engine clears on leave/close. The spread/granular texture visible at end-of-bake is exactly that drifted mass.

Measure both candidate mechanisms in ONE round-trip:
- **(A) bbox flush loss** — at leave/close flush: total wet alpha, wet alpha inside `dryRegionForStroke`, wet alpha outside it (pixel count + alpha sum). Also report whether that outside mass still appears in `exportCompositeCanvas` (dry + display) or is already gone.
- **(B) paper-modulation gap** — transfer-side `clamp(1.4 - ph*0.8, 0.3, 1.4)` vs the display's `paperMod`, summed over the stroke region.

Decision rule: if (A)'s outside-bbox mass is negligible (< 5% of total wet alpha) at leave/close, HALT and report — reopen (B) with the numbers already in hand. Do NOT implement the alpha-model unification (Plan A Task 2) until the mechanism is confirmed by these numbers.
